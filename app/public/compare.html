Four ways to make a KDP puzzle book, compared

People arrive at puzzle books from four directions: a monthly subscription suite, a free browser generator, doing it by hand in Word or Canva, or this tool. Each is the right answer for somebody. Here is what each one actually costs and produces, so you can pick on purpose. Claims about Puzzle Press are checkable on this site; claims about the other routes are kept to what they say about themselves, quoted where it matters.

The free generators have got better, and this page used to describe them as making one puzzle at a time. Several now bulk-generate a whole set with answer keys, free, with no watermark and no account — PuzzleForge is the clearest example. So the line between them and this tool is not how many puzzles you get. It is whether what comes out is puzzles or a book.

When a subscription is the better deal

If you publish a spread of KDP books — planners, journals, colouring books, puzzle books — and put out several a month, a suite with research tools, templates and a cover designer earns its fee. Puzzle Press does one thing. If puzzle books are a sideline in a bigger catalogue, the suite probably wins.

If puzzle books are the thing, the arithmetic goes the other way. A subscription is paid for every month you are publishing, including the months you are only fixing typos — Book Bolt's annual Pro plan is $239.88 a year; $19 once is $19 once. And the suite still leaves you to assemble the book and check the geometry, which is the part that gets manuscripts rejected.

When a free generator is enough

For a single puzzle — a classroom worksheet, a party favour, a page in a newsletter — a free generator is exactly right, and Puzzle Press is overkill. The trouble starts at fifty: fifty images to place, fifty solutions to keep in order, page numbers, a divider, four notes pages, an even count, the gutter swapping sides, fonts embedded. That is not puzzle work; it is layout work, and it is where the free route stops being free.

When by hand is right

A hand-made book can be anything: illustrated mazes, themed pages, a story between puzzles. Generators cannot do that and this one does not try. If you go this way, the guide has every KDP rule with the numbers, and the margin and spine calculators give you the exact figures for your page count. They are free and do not need the tool.

Whichever route you take, three things get files rejected: a gutter that does not grow with the page count (or sits on the same side of every page), fonts not embedded in the PDF, and a cover whose spine was calculated with 0.06" added — a hardcover rule that several popular calculators apply to paperbacks. The full list is in the guide.

What Puzzle Press does not do

Dense newspaper-style crosswords, cryptograms, dot-to-dot, colouring pages: no. Themed crosswords with clues and criss-cross fill-ins: yes. Illustrated or themed page designs: no. Journals, planners, anything that is not a puzzle book: no. Cover artwork beyond your title on a colour, with two of the book's own puzzles on it: no — many publishers make their cover elsewhere and use the spine figure from here. It makes word search, sudoku, maze, criss-cross and themed crossword books that are correct and print-ready, and stops there.
