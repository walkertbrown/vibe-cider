A warning about other calculators. Several of the top-ranking KDP spine calculators tell you to add 0.06" to the spine. Amazon's own “Create a Paperback Cover” page does not: it says spine width is page count multiplied by paper thickness, and nothing is added. The 0.06" belongs to hardcovers. Adding it to a paperback makes your cover 0.06" wider than KDP expects, and KDP says a cover file "sized at dimensions other than those expected" is caught by its Print Previewer, which will "prevent approval". This page uses Amazon's figure.

The formulas

Spine width = page count × paper thickness. Cover width = 0.125" bleed + back cover + spine + front cover + 0.125" bleed. Cover height = 0.125" + trim height + 0.125".

KDP's help pages don't give a thickness for groundwood paper, only a warning that a cream book over 350 pages, or a white one over 525, needs a new cover if you switch. Their own cover calculator answers it: 0.235" of spine at 100 pages, 1.946" at 828, so 0.00235" a page (checked September 2026). That is thinner than cream and thicker than white. A 300-page book loses 0.045" of spine moving from cream to groundwood, and gains 0.029" moving from white.

Custom trim sizes

KDP prints paperbacks at any custom size from 4" to 8.5" wide and 6" to 11.69" tall, not only the sizes in its menu (KDP help, "Print Options"). The cover formula doesn't change: twice the trim width, plus the spine, plus 0.25" of bleed across, by the trim height plus 0.25". Choose "Custom size" in the calculator above for any of them. These four are from KDP's own cover calculator at 200 pages of cream paper, a 0.5" spine (checked October 2026), and the calculator here gives the same figures:

The trim also sets the printing price. KDP counts a trim as large when it is more than 6.12" wide or more than 9" tall, and a large trim costs more to print per copy (KDP help, "Paperback Printing Cost"). Picture books: 24 and 32 pages in colour

A short colour picture book can be a paperback. KDP's minimums are 24 pages for a paperback in black ink or premium colour, 72 in standard colour, and 75 for any hardcover (KDP help, "Print Options"). Premium colour paper is 0.002347" a page, so the spine is 0.056" at 24 pages and 0.075" at 32: too thin for spine text. KDP's cover calculator gives a spine safe area of 0 at both counts. You don't need Cover Creator for the back. KDP takes your own cover as one PDF with the back, the spine and the front side by side, so the back can be a single picture. KDP adds a barcode to the back unless you supply one; the template above marks its box.

Inside margins change with thickness

The gutter — the inside margin, against the spine — has to grow as a book gets thicker, or the text disappears into the binding. KDP's table: 0.375" up to 150 pages, 0.5" to 300, 0.625" to 500, 0.75" to 700, and 0.875" beyond that. Outside, top and bottom need at least 0.25", or 0.375" if you use bleed.

What will it earn?

Spine width settles whether the cover prints. What it earns is a different sum: printing cost depends on page count, ink and trim size, and comes off your royalty. There is a royalty calculator here too — worth a look before you settle on a page count, because every page costs about a penny to print.

Two rules people miss

Spine text needs 80 pages — and then it needs room. KDP's cover help page says "at least 79 pages" in one place and "more than 79 pages" in another, and Cover Creator's help says 80. In October 2026 a book KDP counted at 79 pages had its spine text refused, so plan on 80. Below that, leave the spine blank. From 80 it is allowed, but the text must stay 0.0625" inside each fold: KDP's cover calculator gives the spine's safe area as the spine width less 0.125". An 80-page book on cream paper has a 0.200" spine and a 0.075" safe strip — under 5pt of type. Readable 6pt type needs about 0.22" of spine: 88 pages on cream, 97 on white. Type laid out on exactly 0.0625" puts the bottom of a "g" on the line, so Puzzle Press keeps its spine text a further 1/64" in and starts printing it at 100 pages on cream, 111 on white. The calculator above shows the safe strip for your book.

Leave the barcode area clear. Keep a 2" × 1.2" clear space in the lower right of the back cover. Amazon prints a barcode there, and artwork underneath it will be covered.
