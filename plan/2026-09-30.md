## 00:10Z — Trace Press: books 10× smaller, 3× faster to make

- **Found:** the 26-page book at 8.5 × 11 was 2.1 MB. The fonts were a few
  KB; all the rest was page content, 80–110 KB per page compressed. That's
  the tracing dots. `page.drawCircle` writes each dot as four
  full-precision Bézier curves, and a page has over a thousand of them.
- **Change (`src/pdf/draw.js`):**
  - Each distinct dot (radius and colour) is drawn once per document as a
    form XObject.
  - Every dot on a page is then a translate and a call to it, with
    positions rounded to 1/1000 pt.
  - It's the same four curves; only where they're stored changes.
- **Measured (same machine, same settings):**
  - 8.5 × 11 free book: 2,098,065 bytes → 207,347.
  - 6 × 9 paid book with 0.45" lines: 1,523,407 bytes → 159,553.
  - Cover: 25,726 → 16,577 bytes.
  - Rendering the three: 3.97 s → 1.33 s.
- **Proved the output unchanged:**
  - Every page of all three was rasterised at 200 DPI before and after.
  - The worst page's total ink differs by 0.023%. That's anti-aliasing from
    the rounding: a few hundred pixels out of 3.7 million, at most 43 grey
    levels.
- **Tests:** new `test/size.test.js`.
  - It sets a 600 KB ceiling on the densest book.
  - It reads back pixels under every dot centre on page 1, because a dot
    that silently stops painting would pass every text test.
  - Mutation check: with the XObject clipped to nothing, it fails (184 of
    1,363 dark). With the change restored, it passes.
  - `npm test` passes 39/39 and `inkcheck` is green (1,248 pages).
  - `browser.mjs` is green live on Chromium and WebKit, and every live
    md5 matches.
