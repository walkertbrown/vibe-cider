## Phase 20 — the promise with nothing behind it

Two places tell a paying customer that a human will fix it:

- `src/worker.js`, when the scan gives up: "email support@bananafest-destiny.com
  and we will unlock it by hand."
- `marketing/support.md`, reply template: "tell me the email on the receipt and
  I will check it against our records and unlock it by hand."

There is no tool that does that. `scripts/` has eleven files and none of them
looks up a payment. The procedure, today, is me improvising against the Stripe
API at whatever hour the email arrives, on the day I am least able to think
clearly. That is not a procedure, it is a hope.

It also matters more than it looks, because of what the unlock actually is: a
record in localStorage with an email and a token, and **nothing validates the
token**. So the fix for a stuck buyer is never "issue them a licence" — there is
nothing to issue. It is always "find the address Stripe has and tell them to
type that one." Which means the whole job is a lookup, and a lookup is a script.

1. **`scripts/unlock.mjs <something>`** — give it an email, a name, a card's
   last four, or a session id, and it searches the live account's completed
   sessions and prints what support needs: the exact address on the receipt
   (which is the string the buyer must type), whether it is actually `paid`,
   when, how much, and whether `/api/verify` will find it. Read-only. It must
   never write to Stripe.
2. **Make it say what to do**, not just what it found. The output should end
   with the sentence I would paste into the reply.
3. **Test it against the live account** — including the case that brings people
   to support in the first place: an address that is not there.
4. **Walk every claim in `marketing/support.md`** and cut or fix any I cannot
   actually execute. A reply template that promises something I cannot do is
   worse than no template.

## Phase 21 — the cold visitor (added 02:42, before the work)

Every suite I have checks that the product *works*: a book renders, a cover
sizes, the watermark is there, the unlock finds a payment. Not one of them
checks whether a person who has never heard of this arrives from a Product Hunt
link and gets to a finished book without being told what to do.

That is the only gap left that can cost a sale tomorrow, and tonight is the
last window in which I am willing to change the site. Anything found after this
I am not touching mid-launch — I would rather ship a known rough edge than
deploy on a day I cannot roll back calmly.

What I am actually testing, as a fresh browser with no storage:

1. Land on `/` the way PH sends people — cold, no referrer knowledge, phone
   width as well as desktop.
2. **Time to first understanding.** Is it clear in one screen what this makes
   and that it is free to try? Without scrolling, on a 390px phone.
3. **Time to a made book, doing only what the page suggests.** No knowledge of
   presets, no reading the docs. If the shortest honest path to a downloaded
   PDF needs a decision I have not explained, that is the bug.
4. **Does the free/paid boundary read as fair or as bait?** The watermark is
   the whole business model. A visitor who feels tricked at the download step
   does not buy, and says so publicly on the thread.
5. Anything that only I know because I built it.

Fix only what is cheap and safe. Write down what is not, and leave it.
