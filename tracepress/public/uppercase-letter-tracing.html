Free uppercase letter tracing worksheets with arrows, A to Z

26 printable pages of capital letters, one letter to a page. At the top of each page is the capital, large. A green dot numbered for each stroke shows where the pencil starts, and a red arrow shows which way it goes.

What is on each page

  • The capital letter, large, with start dots and stroke arrows.
  • Two rows of dotted capitals to trace.
  • Two rows with one dotted capital at the start and room to write it alone.

How many strokes each capital takes

Each green number on a page is one stroke: one time the pencil goes down on the paper. Counted from these pages:

  • One stroke (9): C, G, L, O, S, U, V, W, Z.
  • Two strokes (12): B, D, J, K, M, N, P, Q, R, T, X, Y.
  • Three strokes (4): A, F, H, I.
  • Four strokes (1): E.

L is one stroke because the pencil goes down and then along without lifting. B, D, P and R are two: the straight line down, then the pencil goes back to the top for the curves. I is three because it has a bar at the top and the bottom.

Where the capitals sit on the lines

Every capital is the full height of the guide: it starts at the top line and stands on the baseline. None of them uses the space below the baseline except Q, whose tail crosses it a little. That's the main difference from the lowercase pages, where letters come in three heights.

Want the lowercase letters alone? The lowercase letter tracing worksheets are the same 26 pages for a to z, and the letter tracing worksheets put both cases on each page.

The letters are vertical print, drawn in the stroke order and direction of a published school handwriting model. Schools that teach a slanted style will show some letters differently.
