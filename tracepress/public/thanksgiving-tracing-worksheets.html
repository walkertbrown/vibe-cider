Free Thanksgiving tracing worksheets

Twenty Thanksgiving words, one a page, to print and trace. Each page has a picture of the word to colour in, the word large with numbered start dots and arrows on every letter, then rows of it in dots to trace on four-line guides, then lines to write it alone.

Download the worksheets (PDF, 20 pages)

8.5" × 11", no sign-up. The words, in order: mug, cake, bowl, home, bread, wheat, maple, glass, apple, acorn, carrot, cherry, candle, teapot, pepper, cookie, feather, pumpkin, seedling, mushroom. Every word is a thing a child can see in its picture. They run from three letters to eight, so the early pages suit a child just starting and the later ones a child who is writing words already.

Which letters the words practise

Between them, the twenty words use 21 of the 26 lowercase letters. The five they leave out are j, q, v, x and z. To practise every letter, add jacket, quilt, van, box and zeppelin to the words in the book maker: each has a picture, and each brings in one of the missing letters.

Tall letters and tails

On the four-line guides, seven lowercase letters rise to the headline: b, d, f, h, k, l and t. Five drop below the baseline: g, j, p, q and y. The other fourteen sit between the baseline and the dashed midline, apart from the dot on an i. One of these words stays between the baseline and the midline all the way: acorn. Every letter in it is the same height. Seventeen of these words reach up to the headline: cake (k), bowl (b and l), home (h), bread (b and d), wheat (h and t), maple (l), glass (l), apple (l), carrot (t), cherry (h), candle (d and l), teapot (t), cookie (k), feather (f, t and h), pumpkin (k), seedling (d and l) and mushroom (h). Nine of these words drop below the baseline: mug (g), maple (p), glass (g), apple (p), cherry (y), teapot (p), pepper (p), pumpkin (p) and seedling (g).

The words by length and strokes

A stroke is one movement of the pencil, from putting it down to lifting it, and each letter on the page shows its strokes with numbered start dots. An i or j takes two: the line, then the dot. These words run from 3 to 9 strokes, 124 in all.

Ordered by strokes instead, fewest first, the words go mug, bowl, home, cake, bread, maple, glass, apple, acorn, wheat, candle, pepper, carrot, cherry, teapot, cookie, mushroom, feather, pumpkin and seedling. Words with the same count keep their order above. The biggest change is wheat: it has five letters but takes 6 strokes, as t takes two, so it moves from sixth to tenth. To make the book in that order, open Trace Press with the words by strokes.

Selling a Thanksgiving tracing book on KDP

Trace Press makes the whole book: A to Z, then these words a page each, with a matching full-wrap cover, in any of six KDP trim sizes. The button below opens it with the twenty words already typed in, for a 46-page book. Change the words, add numbers 0 to 9 or a “This book belongs to” page, and download. For the cover's size at that page count, see tracing book cover size. There are Halloween and Christmas tracing worksheets too, made the same way.

A word gets a picture when Trace Press has one for it: 163 everyday words so far, such as dog, train and umbrella. All twenty pictures on this page are from Tabler Icons, under the MIT licence. One word uses a picture with another name: maple is a maple leaf. Pumpkin is Tabler's carved pumpkin with the face taken out and two ribs drawn in, so it suits an autumn book as well as a Halloween one. Words you add get theirs from the same two sets, except quilt, which Trace Press drew. Both allow copies to be sold. The licence texts: Tabler Icons, Lucide.
