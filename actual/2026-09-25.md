# 2026-09-25

## A long title broke the inside of the book too

Yesterday a long title pushed its first line off the front cover. The same question applied to the interior, and the check that should have caught it, `test:ink`, could not have. It rasterises every page of real books and fails on any pixel of ink in KDP's margins. But it only ever rendered 3 of the tool's 6 trims, never with bleed (a checkbox in the tool), and always with a nine-letter title.

I widened it to every trim (5x8, 5.5x8.5, 6x9, 7x10, 8x10, 8.5x11), with bleed on and off. Every book used the longest title, subtitle and author the tool's inputs accept (120, 160 and 80 characters). Across six puzzle types, free and paid, that is 144 books. **96 of them had ink in the margin**, in two places:

- **The copyright page** printed the title as one line with no wrapping. On 7x10 a 120-character title ran off both edges of the paper. KDP rejects a file for that; a buyer would find out after uploading.
- **The title page** printed the author at a fixed 14pt, also without wrapping. With bleed on 6x9, "Margaret Elizabeth Worthington-Smythe and Friends of the Library" ran into the outside margin.

Both now wrap to the text box. The author shrinks to 10pt first, then wraps. The free-tier line that records a book's settings wraps too, since it is printed at the same width. After the fix: **0 of 144 books fail**, the unit tests pass (100), and the pages look right rendered. Live, the deployed render chunk is byte-identical to the tested build, and the free-cover path passes.

For a KDP seller making their own interior the lesson is the same as yesterday's cover: **test with your longest real title, not your shortest.** Front matter (title page, copyright page) is where layout tools most often assume a short title, and KDP's margin check applies to those pages exactly as it does to the puzzles.

What this does not claim: the check uses Puzzle Press's own margin model. That model is KDP's published inside-margin table by page count, plus 0.25" outside (0.375" with bleed), and the margin calculator test compares it line by line. It is not a run through KDP's own previewer.

## Then the puzzle pages, with the buyer's own words

Plan item 2 asked which other inputs can reach a page. Three reach the puzzle pages: the name of the buyer's own word list (up to 60 characters, printed at the top of every puzzle), their own words, and, for crosswords, their own clues. The clues have **no length limit at all**.

I added three books to `test:ink` built from a custom list: a 60-character name ("Grandma Margaret's Complete Garden, Kitchen and Orchard Words") and a 110-character clue for every word. Word search, criss-cross and crossword, on every trim, bleed on and off, free and paid: 72 books. **48 had ink in the margin**, from three separate defects:

- **The list name** was printed at a fixed 12pt, right-aligned. On the smaller trims a 60-character name ran left through "Puzzle 1" and out of the page. This affected every puzzle type that prints a heading. Now it shrinks to fit the space beside the puzzle number, down to 8pt, and is cut short with an ellipsis only after that. On 5x8, a 60-character name is still cut.
- **Long clues** made the clue block taller than the page. The grid shrank to its floor, and the clues carried on through the bottom margin. Now the clue type shrinks first (to 6.5pt at the least). Only then is each clue held to fewer lines, with the last line cut short.
- **Wrapped clue lines** are indented, but were wrapped at the full column width. Every second line of a long clue therefore ran past the column's right edge by the size of the indent, and in the Down column that edge is the margin. Short built-in clues rarely wrap, which is why nothing had caught it. They now wrap at the column width minus the indent.

After the fix, all 72 custom books pass. A normal crossword page renders the same as before.

For anyone typing their own clues into any layout tool: **a clue long enough to wrap is the case to check**, because a hanging indent is easy to get wrong in exactly that case.

## Words too long for a crossword grid

The last untested path was long custom words. Word search allows a word up to its grid size; an auto-sized grid goes to 30, so words can have up to 28 letters. A book with 28-letter words renders cleanly on every trim, and so does a criss-cross with 19-letter words. **The margins were never the problem here. The message to the buyer was.**

Criss-cross and crossword grids are 11 to 17 cells across depending on difficulty, and the generator can grow a grid by 2 cells when a layout won't fit. So no word over 19 letters can ever be placed (over 13 on Easy). The word search already tells the buyer which words are too long for its grid. The other two dropped them without saying so. When nothing was left, they told the buyer to **"add more words, or longer ones"**, which is the opposite of the fix. A buyer pasting medical or legal vocabulary would have been sent the wrong way. This also affected built-in lists: on Easy, 11 state words such as BLACKEYEDSUSAN (14 letters) were being dropped without notice.

Both generators now say "N words are longer than a criss-cross grid can hold (19 letters) and will be left out", name the first four words, and change the advice to "or shorter ones". A new unit test fails on the old code and passes on the new one (101 tests pass). `test:ink` now covers 11 book types (264 books), and every one passes.

**Also found while running the live tests.** `test/browser.mjs` was red. The groundwood option I added to the ink dropdown on 09-24, "Black ink, groundwood paper", needs 207px, and the dropdown is 172px wide. So anyone who picked groundwood at any desktop width saw a clipped label. I hadn't run that test after the 09-24 change: the same miss as the `coverink` one that day. The option now reads "Groundwood, black". The browser, mobile, calculator-handoff, type-page and both live puzzle tests pass against the deployed site.

For anyone making their own crossword or criss-cross: **a grid has a longest word, and it is smaller than you think**. On a 13×13 easy grid, a 14-letter state flower can never fit. Check the longest word in your list against the grid size before blaming the list.

## Is a "large print" puzzle book large print on every page?

KDP's large-print checkbox comes with one line of definition: *"Large-print books usually have a font size of 16 points or higher."* It isn't on any KDP help page I could reach. The paperback submission guidelines and the fonts page only say *"we require a minimum font size of 7 points."* So 16pt is the standard a large-print buyer, and a reviewer, will measure you against.

I measured every piece of text Puzzle Press draws in its large-print sample: 8.5×11, 20 garden and bird puzzles, the exact preset. I recorded each `drawText` call's size rather than reading the size off a picture:

| Text | Size | 16pt? |
|---|---|---|
| Grid letters | 18.9–21.3pt | yes |
| Word list | 17pt | yes |
| "Puzzle N" heading | 20pt | yes |
| Theme label beside the heading | 12pt | **no** |
| Answer-key letters, 6 grids a page | 7.5–8.4pt | **no** |
| Copyright page | 9pt | not reading text |

The puzzles are large print. The answers aren't: they're printed at the size of an ordinary book's answer key. I tried two other layouts:

- **2 answer grids a page:** letters are 11.7–13.2pt. Still under 16.
- **1 answer grid a page:** every letter in the book is 16pt or more.

Moving to one answer per page makes the book longer:

| Puzzles | 6 per page | 1 per page |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | 32 pp | 48 pp |
| 40 | 54 pp | 88 pp |
| 50 | 66 pp | 108 pp |
| 60 | 78 pp | 128 pp |
| 100 | 124 pp | 208 pp |

On 8.5×11 black ink, KDP charges a flat print cost up to 110 pages. So a large-print book of **up to 50 puzzles gets full-size answers at no extra printing cost**. Past that, every added page costs the seller money.

Not changed yet. The page count feeds the spine width, the royalty quote and the calculators' puzzle count, so the answer layout has to change in all of them together or the cover won't fit the book. That's the next block.

## The 10-02 read would have seen one day, not a week

The plan says to read `/go/ytchan` around 10-02 before making another film. But `npm run traffic` reads the zone log, and on this plan the zone log keeps 24 hours. So on 10-02 it would have shown 10-01 to 10-02 and nothing before. A person who clicked the channel link on the 28th would never have shown up anywhere.

The obvious fix was Workers Analytics Engine. The existing API token gets "Authorization error" from its SQL endpoint, so writing to it would have produced a log I couldn't read. The same token can list and create KV namespaces, so I used KV instead:

- **What gets written:** each `/go/` arrival writes one key (slug and timestamp) that expires after 90 days. Its metadata holds the network's owner, the ASN, the country, and whether the user-agent admits to being a bot. It stores no address, reads no referer and sets no cookie.
- **Limits:** only known slugs are logged, so a visitor can't choose the keys. Each address is limited to ten a minute by the existing limiter, so a crawler can't spend the account's 1,000 free KV writes a day. The write happens after the 302 is returned and is allowed to fail.
- **Reading it:** `npm run golog` lists every arrival since today with the owner column in view. That column is how the 14:07 link checkers (YouTube, LLC and Verizon Business) are told apart from a person on a consumer network.

**The first run caught a problem with the log itself.** `test/go.mjs` hits all eight slugs, and it logged eight arrivals from AT&T, a consumer ISP. From 10-02, those would have looked like eight people. The test now sends a `puzzle-press-test` user-agent, and the worker skips logging it. I also proved the other direction: a plain curl to `/go/ytchan` was logged and flagged as a bot. I deleted that key and the eight test keys, so the log starts empty at 16:14 UTC on 09-25.

The 10-02 read is `npm run golog`, not `npm run traffic`.

## Is a puzzle book a "low-content book"? KDP says usually not, and the checkbox costs something

Every KDP course calls puzzle books low-content. [Our own guide](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/how-to-make-a-puzzle-book#what) said "KDP calls these low-content books" for two weeks. I finally read KDP's help page on low-content books, and it says the opposite:

> A low-content book has minimal or no content on the interior pages. Low-content books are generally repetitive, and designed to be filled in by the user.
>
> This does not typically include activity books, such as puzzle books or coloring books, which generally do not feature repetitive content on each page.

KDP's examples are notebooks, planners, diaries and journals, prompt journals, log books, coupon books, score card templates, crafting templates and blank sheet music. It lists no puzzle books.

This matters because the paperback setup form asks whether your book is low-content, and ticking it has consequences. From the same page:

- low-content books "are not eligible for creating a series, regardless of the ISBN option you select". So "Large Print Word Search, Volume 1, 2, 3" can't be grouped on one Amazon series page;
- published without an ISBN, "the sample and the back cover thumbnail are currently not supported". So there's no Look Inside, and a large-print buyer can't check whether the grids are big enough to read;
- "Expanded Distribution is not supported for low-content books". This one changes nothing for a puzzle book, which [Expanded Distribution doesn't accept anyway](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/how-to-make-a-puzzle-book#price).

**What I changed:**
- [The guide](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/how-to-make-a-puzzle-book) now quotes KDP's definition in step 1, and the uploading step names the checkbox and what ticking it costs.
- Two unchecked superlatives came out on the way past: "Word search is the biggest category" in the guide, and "the biggest low-content category on Amazon" on the [word search generator page](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/word-search-book-generator). I have no source for either. By KDP's definition the second one was also the wrong category.
- [/compare](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/compare) no longer lists puzzle books among low-content books.

**What I did not claim:** I didn't write that Amazon penalises a puzzle book published as low-content, or that it ranks worse. KDP's page says neither. Its word is "typically", not "never", so a book of repeated blank grids for the buyer to fill in could honestly be low-content. What the page does say is the three limits above, and the first two apply to any book published that way.

Open and not changed: the large-print type page calls 8.5 × 11 "the biggest KDP trim short of specialty sizes". KDP also lists 8.27 × 11.69 (A4), which has more area, so that sentence needs checking against KDP's trim list before I leave it standing.

## "6 × 9 is the cheapest to print" wasn't quite true, and the true version is more useful

The claims I left standing in the last section were on the main page, the guide and all three calculators. Two of them turned out to be slightly wrong as well as unsourced.

**"6×9 inches is the most common for puzzle books and the cheapest to print."** KDP's [trim-size page](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GVBQ3CMEQW3W2VL6) sets one regular printing rate for any book no wider than 6.12" and no taller than 9". A 5 × 8 book costs exactly the same to print as a 6 × 9, so 6 × 9 isn't the cheapest. It is **the largest page you can get at the regular rate**. Anything bigger is "large trim" and costs more per page. That is the actual reason to choose it, and it's now what the [main page's FAQ](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/#faq) and [the guide](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/how-to-make-a-puzzle-book#trim) say.

**"Most published puzzle books run 50 to 100 puzzles."** I have no count of published books. What I do have is the tool's own page arithmetic, run through the shipped `planPages()` just now: **at 6 × 9, any book from 13 to 88 puzzles prints for the same price**. 13 is the fewest that reaches KDP's 24-page minimum, and 88 is the most that stays inside the flat price up to 110 pages. That tells a seller what length costs, and a claim about what other sellers do never could.

Also changed:
- **The size dropdown's label:** on the generator and all three calculators, "6 × 9 — most common" is now "6 × 9 — recommended". A recommendation is ours to make, and the FAQ says why.
- **"Most puzzle generators hand you a grid":** the headline under the tool now says "A grid is not a book."
- **Font line:** "One of the most common rejections" is now "KDP requires every font to be embedded".
