## What word search covers on Amazon have in common, and the cover generator rebuilt around it

The boss looked at our covers and said: "It's a word search the covers need
fun". They were right, and it can be checked. I pulled the first 24 covers
Amazon's book search returns for "word search books for adults" and "large
print word search" and laid them side by side with ours.

Five of the 24 aren't comparable: three are multi-book packs and two
(Murdle, Murdoku) aren't word search. My count from the thumbnails of the
other 19:
- **10 set the title stacked and heavy in two or three colours**, usually
  white plus yellow or red, often over a shadow or an outline. Ours was plain
  white sans.
- **11 fill the background with a letter grid** loud enough to read as one at
  thumbnail size. Ours was a tint that vanished in a thumbnail.
- **10 carry a round sticker with a number**: "224 PUZZLES", "2000+ WORDS",
  "LARGE PRINT". Ours put the count in a strip at the bottom.
- **Several of the loud ones ring their answers in more than one colour.** At
  213 pixels wide I can't count that reliably. Ours ringed three words in one.

The other half is a calm style: Brain Games' spiral editions, a Smithsonian
botanical, illustrated cabins and lighthouses. That look is built on
illustration, which a generator with no stock art can't do honestly. The loud
style is type, colour and the grid itself, which is exactly what this code
already draws.

What changed in the generator:
- A title face with character: Lilita One (SIL Open Font License, embedded
  and subset like the rest). The lines alternate white and the colour's accent,
  each over a drop shadow. A title in characters Lilita doesn't have
  (Cyrillic, Greek) falls back to the old bold face rather than failing.
- The front puzzle circles its five longest answers in five colours. Sudoku
  pencils its first cells in the same colours.
- A word search wrap has found-word loops scattered across its background
  letter grid, never across the spine.
- The front puzzle card sits at a 3° tilt, shrunk so the turned card takes no
  more room than a straight one.
- A starburst sticker on the card's corner carries the puzzle count.

The KDP rules didn't move. `test/coversafe.mjs` renders 198 covers (three
trims, all eight colours, free and paid, deliberately over-long titles and
authors, plus the published samples) and finds no text within 0.125" of a
trim line. `test/coverink.mjs` finds the barcode area still clear, and spine
text only where KDP's spine safe area has room for it.

One test did go red, and it was the test that was wrong. `test/fontwarm.mjs`
checks that the fonts load when a person first touches the tool and not
before. It expected exactly "2" fonts, typed out, and the cover now has a
third. It now counts the files in `public/fonts/`.

## "1 PUZZLES", and a title that ended on a lone "3"

A sample cover only shows the cases someone chose for it. So I rendered the
ones nobody picks for a sample: a book of one puzzle, and a 200-puzzle large
print book called "Large Print Word Search for Seniors: Volume 3", with a
subtitle and a three-part author name. Every puzzle type, a Cyrillic title, and

text. With the balancing taken out it fails, so it would catch this coming

## The same stranded word, inside the book

The cover fix left a question: does the inside of the book break titles the
same way? It did. The first two pages of every book are a title page and a
copyright page, and both wrapped long text greedily, filling each line before
starting the next. A 5×8 book called "The Enormous Christmas Holiday Word

"Family" alone. Its title page ran "…Collection for the" / "Whole Family". A
large print book's title page broke as "…for Seniors:" / "Volume 3".

the number of lines doesn't change. Each line is set to the narrowest width
that still fits, so the lines come out close in length:

plain wrapping, because their second lines hang indented under the first.

A test renders that Christmas title and fails if any line of it is a single

## "Café" printed as CAF

A word search buyer can paste their own words. Before a word goes into a grid
it is reduced to the letters A to Z. Until today that step deleted any letter
with an accent instead of converting it, and nothing warned about it. So a
recipe book's word list printed "Café" as **CAF**, "Jalapeño" as **JALAPEO**,
"Crème brûlée" as **CRMEBRLE** and "Piñata" as **PIATA**, and the grid hid
the misspelled word, ready to upload. None of the 90 built-in word lists has
an accent, which is why no sample or test ever showed it.

Now each accented letter becomes its plain letter: CAFE, JALAPENO,
CREMEBRULEE, PINATA. A few letters don't split into letter plus accent, so
they are mapped by hand: Æbleskiver becomes AEBLESKIVER, Œuvre OEUVRE,
Smørrebrød SMORREBROD and Łódź LODZ. Straße already came out as STRASSE. A
word in another alphabet, such as Cyrillic, is still left out, as before,
because the grid only has A to Z. A crossword's own clue follows its word
through the same step, so "Café — coffee shop" still finds its clue.

Two tests cover it. A unit test checks each of those words, and it fails with
the fix removed. The browser test now types "café, jalapeño" into the live
page and reads the PDF it downloads. It failed against the site before this
deploy and passes after it.

## A flower in the title printed as an empty box

The book's fonts cover English and most European languages, Greek and
Cyrillic. They have no emoji and no Chinese, Japanese or Korean. A title
typed as "Grandma's Puzzles 🌸" came out of both the interior and the cover
with an empty rectangle where the flower was, and "数独パズル Sudoku" as five
rectangles and a word. The page said nothing about it. A buyer would find
out from the proof copy, or not at all.

Now anything the fonts can't draw is left out of the PDF, and the page says
what: "Left out of the PDF: 🌸. The book's fonts can't print it, and KDP
would have got an empty box." The fix sits where every piece of text passes
before it is drawn, so it covers titles, subtitles, author names, your own
list names and your own crossword clues in one place. Centring is measured on
the text that is actually printed. An emoji used to push the whole cover
title out of the display face into the plain one; now "Family ❤️ Puzzles"
keeps the display face, and curly quotes, which that face does have, were
never the problem.

The test renders a title full of emoji and Chinese next to the same title
without them, and requires the two pages to be identical pixel for pixel. The
first version of the test compared extracted text, and it still passed with
the fix removed, because a box has no text to extract. The pixel version
fails with the fix removed. The browser test now types "Fruit Bowl 🍎" on the
live page and waits for the note. It failed before this deploy and passes
after it. The cover safe-area, ink, line-weight and margin checks all pass.

## "4th of July" printed as THOFJULY

This is the same step as the accents, with a different kind of character. A
puzzle grid holds the letters A to Z, so digits were deleted from a pasted
word, and whatever was left went into the book. A July book's list printed
"4th of July" as **THOFJULY**, "R2D2" as **RD** and "7-Up" as **UP**, and
hid them in the grid. A word in another alphabet, "Борщ", was deleted
entirely. None of this raised a warning.

Now a word with a digit is left out rather than cut down, and every word left
out is named above the preview:

> Left out: "4th of July", "7-Up", "R2D2", "Борщ", "a". A puzzle word needs
> at least two letters, and a grid holds only A to Z, so numbers and other
> alphabets can't go in as typed. Spell numbers out ("Fourth of July").

Word search, criss-cross and crossword books all show it. The 90 built-in
word lists raise no warning, and their pages are unchanged. A test feeds
those words to the generator and checks that they are left out and named. It
fails with the digit rule removed. The browser test now pastes "7-Up" into
the live page and waits for the note, and checks the PDF has no UP in it. It
failed before this deploy and passes after it.
