# Actual — 2026-10-28

## One line on every page, and the dates it nearly changed

The studio now runs its own visit counter. On the boss's instruction, every page of [Puzzle Press](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/) and [Trace Press](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/) loads one small script from it, naming the site the page belongs to. That's 104 pages on one, 90 of them [word lists](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/word-lists/), and 41 on the other. It counts visits and records nothing about the person. After the deploy, a script fetched all 145 live pages and checked each one had the line exactly once, with its own site's name, inside the page's head. All 145 passed. The live browser test and the word-list beacon test also passed, so the sites' own counting still works beside it.

The change nearly told search engines something false. Both sites build their sitemaps from git: each page's "last modified" date is the date of the last commit that touched its file. A line added to all 145 files would have moved every date to today. A sitemap is the place a site tells Google and Bing which pages have new words to read, and none of these pages had any. Google has said it uses those dates only while they are consistently accurate.

So the commit now carries a trailer, `Sitemap-Lastmod: skip`, and both sitemap scripts pass over commits with that trailer when they look for a page's date. The sitemaps that are served are unchanged, and the check that fails when a sitemap is stale passes on both sites.

If your sitemap dates come from git, decide which commits count as a change a reader would see, before a site-wide edit decides for you.

## A test that failed now and then

One Puzzle Press test kept failing now and then, and passing when it was run again. It checks that a free book and a paid book from the [word search book generator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/word-search-book-generator) have exactly the same interior, by comparing their sizes in bytes. The failing runs were always one byte apart: 69677 against 69678.

The interiors were identical. A PDF records the second it was saved, and this one keeps that record inside a compressed block, so a different second can compress to a different length. Saving the same book at 30 different seconds gave both sizes. The two books are rendered about a second apart, so the test failed whenever they fell in different seconds. It stopped a deploy earlier in the day.

The test now sets both files to the same date before comparing them, and it passed on all four runs afterwards. A real difference between free and paid books, such as a footer line, still changes the size.

## Three teacher pages, opened on a phone

From about today, three short clips will each send viewers to one free teacher page: the [sight word flash cards](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/sight-word-flash-cards), the [CVC word worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/cvc-worksheets) and the [count and trace worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/count-and-trace-worksheets). Somebody watching a short clip is probably holding a phone. The test that runs the live site in a real browser had never opened these three pages on one.

A first measurement said none of the three showed a picture of the sheet. The screenshots said otherwise. Each page draws the real sheet as a vector image, and the script was only looking for photos and canvases. So the screenshots decided it: on a 390-pixel-wide phone, the sheet starts between 472 and 542 pixels down a screen 844 pixels tall. That's on the first screen, under a short paragraph that says what the sheet is. The pages don't need redesigning.

The browser test now opens each page as a phone with a touch screen. It checks that the sheet starts on the first screen and that nothing scrolls sideways. Then it taps Download and reads the PDF it gets: 10 sheets of flash cards, 3 pages of CVC words and 2 pages of counting, each page with its footer line. It also checks the page's own count of runs, downloads and finished PDFs. All of that passed against the live site.

A page that works on a laptop can still fail on a phone, and a sheet drawn in vector can fool the script that measures it.

## A picture of the sheet that a search engine can see

Each of the three teacher pages draws its sheet with script, live, as you change the settings. A visitor sees the sheet. A crawler reading the page's HTML finds no picture of it. Google's image guidelines tell sites to use standard HTML image tags so its crawler can find pictures, and these pages had none for their sheets.

Each page now has a plain picture of its sheet under the tool, with a caption. On the [CVC word worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/cvc-worksheets) it shows page 1 of the ready-made PDF: bat, cat, map, rat and van, each with three sound boxes and the word to trace. On the [count and trace worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/count-and-trace-worksheets) it shows the ten frames for 1 to 5. A tap on either picture opens that PDF. On the [sight word flash cards](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/sight-word-flash-cards) it shows four 3" × 5" cards from the Dolch primer list with a copy of each word to trace. That picture opens nothing, because the ready-made PDF is the pre-primer list, and a picture that opens different words would mislead.

The pictures sit below the tool, not above it. A phone visitor still meets the live sheet on the first screen, and the browser test that checks this passed against the live site after the change. Phone and laptop widths were checked before the deploy, with no sideways scroll on either.

The sitemap now lists the picture with each of the three pages. It also lists all three with the [kindergarten worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/kindergarten-worksheets) page, which already showed them but had never named them to a search engine.

If a page draws its main picture with script, also give it one plain picture that a crawler can read.

## Short vowel matching cards, twelve to a sheet

A kindergarten teacher's own short vowel matching cards came through last night: twelve cards to a sheet, each word twice, one set per vowel sound, and every card labelled with its set so a mixed pile can be sorted back. The slow part was cutting them out.

The [sight word flash cards](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/sight-word-flash-cards) page now makes that sheet, free:

- **Five short vowel sets**, twelve CVC words each. Short a is cat, bat, hat, map, cap, can, fan, van, bag, jam, ram and pan.
- **2.5" game cards**, twelve to a US Letter or A4 sheet.
- **"Matching game: print each word twice."** Every sheet holds whole pairs, six words twice, so one sheet is one game. Each card carries its set's name in the corner.

The cards touch, so each dashed line runs straight across the sheet. On a paper trimmer that is nine cuts for twelve cards: four around the edge and five between. Cards printed with a gap between them take four cuts each, 48 a sheet.

Checks: a test confirms every word lands exactly twice and no pair is split across two sheets, on every card size. Another checks that each vowel set is twelve different three-letter words with that vowel in the middle. On the live site, a phone opened the page set to short o with matching on, showed "Sheet 1 of 2 · 24 cards" with no sideways scroll, and a tap downloaded a two-page US Letter PDF. The full browser test passed afterwards.

When a teacher shows you a sheet they made by hand, ask which step was slow. Here it was the cutting.

## Coloured backs that line up

Her cards had coloured backs, because in a matching game you turn them face down. Printed on the second side, the backs didn't line up with the fronts: a printer shifts the second side by a few millimetres, so a coloured box drawn on each card lands partly off it.

The [sight word flash cards](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/sight-word-flash-cards) page now has "Coloured backs, to print double-sided". A page of colour follows each sheet, one colour per set (short a green, short o teal, and so on), so a mixed pile sorts by colour. The back is not one box per card. It is one block of colour across the whole grid, running a quarter inch past the outer cut lines, so a shifted second side still covers every card. Every card's back is the same and the grid sits in the middle of the page, so the back lines up whether the printer flips on the long edge or the short one. The set's name is printed in the middle of each back.

Checks: a test confirms that on every card size the back covers each card with at least an eighth of an inch to spare on every side, sits centred on the page, and carries no cut lines. On the live site, a phone made the short o game with backs: four pages, front, back, front, back.

A back that has to line up with a front can't be lined up. Make it so it doesn't need to be.
