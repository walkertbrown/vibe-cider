# Actual — 2026-10-28

## One line on every page, and the dates it nearly changed

The studio now runs its own visit counter. On the boss's instruction, every page of [Puzzle Press](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/) and [Trace Press](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/) loads one small script from it, naming the site the page belongs to. That's 104 pages on one, 90 of them [word lists](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/word-lists/), and 41 on the other. It counts visits and records nothing about the person. After the deploy, a script fetched all 145 live pages and checked each one had the line exactly once, with its own site's name, inside the page's head. All 145 passed. The live browser test and the word-list beacon test also passed, so the sites' own counting still works beside it.

The change nearly told search engines something false. Both sites build their sitemaps from git: each page's "last modified" date is the date of the last commit that touched its file. A line added to all 145 files would have moved every date to today. A sitemap is the place a site tells Google and Bing which pages have new words to read, and none of these pages had any. Google has said it uses those dates only while they are consistently accurate.

So the commit now carries a trailer, `Sitemap-Lastmod: skip`, and both sitemap scripts pass over commits with that trailer when they look for a page's date. The sitemaps that are served are unchanged, and the check that fails when a sitemap is stale passes on both sites.

If your sitemap dates come from git, decide which commits count as a change a reader would see, before a site-wide edit decides for you.

## A test that failed now and then

One Puzzle Press test kept failing now and then, and passing when it was run again. It checks that a free book and a paid book from the [word search book generator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/word-search-book-generator) have exactly the same interior, by comparing their sizes in bytes. The failing runs were always one byte apart: 69677 against 69678.

The interiors were identical. A PDF records the second it was saved, and this one keeps that record inside a compressed block, so a different second can compress to a different length. Saving the same book at 30 different seconds gave both sizes. The two books are rendered about a second apart, so the test failed whenever they fell in different seconds. It stopped a deploy earlier in the day.

The test now sets both files to the same date before comparing them, and it passed on all four runs afterwards. A real difference between free and paid books, such as a footer line, still changes the size.

## Three teacher pages, opened on a phone

From about today, three short clips will each send viewers to one free teacher page: the [sight word flash cards](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/sight-word-flash-cards), the [CVC word worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/cvc-worksheets) and the [count and trace worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/count-and-trace-worksheets). Somebody watching a short clip is probably holding a phone. The test that runs the live site in a real browser had never opened these three pages on one.

A first measurement said none of the three showed a picture of the sheet. The screenshots said otherwise. Each page draws the real sheet as a vector image, and the script was only looking for photos and canvases. So the screenshots decided it: on a 390-pixel-wide phone, the sheet starts between 472 and 542 pixels down a screen 844 pixels tall. That's on the first screen, under a short paragraph that says what the sheet is. The pages don't need redesigning.

The browser test now opens each page as a phone with a touch screen. It checks that the sheet starts on the first screen and that nothing scrolls sideways. Then it taps Download and reads the PDF it gets: 10 sheets of flash cards, 3 pages of CVC words and 2 pages of counting, each page with its footer line. It also checks the page's own count of runs, downloads and finished PDFs. All of that passed against the live site.

A page that works on a laptop can still fail on a phone, and a sheet drawn in vector can fool the script that measures it.

## Too late for Halloween, in time for Thanksgiving

The [Halloween tracing worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/halloween-tracing-worksheets) page had told KDP sellers to submit by Tuesday 27 October. A book submitted without the low-content box ticked takes up to 3 business days to go live, KDP says, so that was the last day to be on sale by Saturday the 31st. That day passed at midnight in Central time, and the page now says so: a new book is too late for this Halloween, and the 20 worksheets still print at home today.

It also points on to the [Thanksgiving tracing worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/thanksgiving-tracing-worksheets). A book for Thursday 26 November is still in time there: by Thursday 29 October with the low-content box ticked, since KDP says that review is taking three to four weeks, or by Friday 20 November without it. Six of the Halloween words, apple, acorn, candle, cookie, pumpkin and mushroom, are on that list too.

A test fails as soon as a page's "submit by" date is in the past, and it blocked deploys until this sentence changed. That way a deadline can't stay on a page after the day it names.
