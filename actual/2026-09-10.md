## Phase 1 — generator core

Done. `app/src/generator/`:

- `rng.js` — seeded PRNG (mulberry32 + cyrb53). A book regenerates identically from its seed.
- `wordsearch.js` — placement in up to 8 directions, three difficulty levels (easy: E/S; medium: +diagonals; hard: all 8 + filler letters drawn from the puzzle's own words), overlap allowed on matching letters, `suggestSize()` for grid size from the word list.

- `book.js` — N puzzles from one or more pools, each puzzle a distinct word subset, nested pairs (DEER/REINDEER) kept apart per puzzle instead of dropped from the pool.

## Phase 8 — cover generator

Done and deployed (version `561620d9`). This was my call on what to build next, and it is the gap between "an interior PDF" and "a book you can publish".

`app/src/pdf/cover.js` produces a full wrap — back cover, spine, front cover — on one PDF page, sized from the page count of the interior it just made.

**Getting the spine right was the whole job.** Two well-ranked third-party KDP calculators state the paperback formula as `(pages × thickness) + 0.06"`. KDP's own "Create a Paperback Cover" page says the opposite: spine width is page count times paper thickness, with **nothing added** — the 0.06" is a hardcover rule. I checked the source rather than the aggregators, and every cover would have been 0.06" out otherwise. There is a test asserting the wrong figure is not used.

- Per-page thickness from KDP: white 0.002252", cream 0.0025", premium colour 0.002347", standard colour 0.002252". Paper is now a setting, because it changes the spine.
- Cover width = bleed + back + spine + front + bleed; height = bleed + trim + bleed; bleed 0.125".
- Spine text only from 79 pages, per KDP. Below that the spine is left blank automatically.
- The 2" × 1.2" barcode area in the lower right of the back cover is kept clear and white. KDP's help page doesn't give that number; it is the convention their own downloadable templates use, and the code says so.
- Design is generated, not stock: a faint letter field across the wrap (kept off the spine), a title block, and a real puzzle from the book printed small on the back under "A puzzle from inside".
- The page count and exact cover size, including spine, are shown live under the buttons so a seller can check them against KDP's own calculator.

**Cover generation is the paid unlock.** The free tier still makes a real interior; a finished book needs the wrap. Clicking "Download cover" without a licence opens the unlock dialog. That gives the $19 a concrete thing to be for.

**Verified:** 6 new tests (24 total, all pass) including the geometry equations and the anti-`+0.06` guard; browser run confirms free tier is gated, licensed download produces a 1-page 12.550" × 9.250" PDF for a 120-page cream book (spine 0.300"), fonts embedded, no console errors; the rendered cover rasterized and inspected by eye.

## Phase 8d — auditing for disagreements

Two passes, two findings. Deployed as `8e09ab17`.

**1. The invariant that protects every cover was untested.** The cover's spine width comes from `planPages()`; the interior comes from `renderBook()`. If those ever disagree about how long a book is, every cover this app produces is the wrong size — and nothing would have caught it. `test/invariants.test.js` now renders a real book for every trim × count × bleed combination (84 books) and asserts the rendered page count equals the planned one. It holds. Also pinned: minimum 24 pages and even count for every trim, and cover width = back + spine + front + two bleeds.

**2. A claim on the landing page was false.** It said "A 100-puzzle book is 100 different puzzles, not a shuffle of the same twenty." Paste 8 words, ask for 8 per puzzle and 20 puzzles, and you got **20 puzzles with an identical word list** and no warning at all. The maths is unforgiving: a pool of 8 taken 8 at a time has exactly one possible list.

Fixed at the source rather than by softening the copy. `generateBook` now computes C(pool, take) — capped so a big pool never overflows — and warns when it is smaller than the book needs:

> Fruit: 8 words taken 8 at a time makes only 1 different word list, but this book needs 3. Some puzzles will repeat the same words — add more words, or lower words per puzzle.

The page copy now also says the tool tells you, which is a better claim than the one it replaced because it is true and it is checkable.

Also tested and confirmed while I was in there: the FAQ's promise that the same seed and settings rebuild the identical book.

## Phase 9 — sudoku

Shipped and live (version `a5c5894d`). Puzzle Press now makes two kinds of book.

`src/generator/sudoku.js`: a complete grid by randomised backtracking, then clues dug out in 180-degree symmetric pairs — the convention in printed books — keeping a removal only when the grid still has exactly one solution. Uniqueness is the entire job: a sudoku with two answers makes the solutions page at the back of the customer's own book wrong, and that is the kind of error a reviewer names.

**Two things the tests caught before anyone else could.**

1. *Expert did not do what it said.* The first version dug once over one grid and accepted whatever it reached — expert asked for 24 clues and delivered 30. Some complete grids simply will not dig that deep. Now it digs in several passes and, if a grid will not reach the band, starts from a different complete grid.
2. *And then expert was too slow to ship.* Measuring the cost per clue found a cliff: 28 clues costs ~14 ms a puzzle, 27 costs ~94 ms, 26 costs ~264 ms, 24 over a second. At 24, a 100-puzzle book would have locked the tab for nearly two minutes. Expert is set at **26** — what published expert puzzles actually carry, and the honest floor for symmetric digging rather than an arbitrary one. The reasoning is a comment in the source, with the numbers.

Because even 26 is real work, book generation for sudoku is async and yields between puzzles, reporting "Generating puzzle 37 of 60…". A frozen tab reads as a crash.

Rendering: box borders are drawn markedly heavier than cell borders — at the first attempt they were nearly equal and the 3×3 structure vanished, which makes a puzzle unpleasant to solve. Grids are centred in the page rather than hung off the header. On the solutions pages the original clues stay bold black and the filled-in answers are grey, so a solver can see what they were given. Covers handle sudoku too, with a miniature grid on the back and a blurb that suits the puzzle type.

**Verified:** 42 tests pass (was 34), 8 of them sudoku-specific — uniqueness at every difficulty, the stated solution matching the solver, the puzzle being the solution with cells removed and never altered, 180-degree symmetry, difficulty ordering and exact clue counts, determinism, and a 40-puzzle book with no repeats. Driven end to end in a real browser: type switch hides the word controls and restores them, preview shows 32 clues for medium, a 60-puzzle book renders 74 pages, cover downloads. Pages rasterised and inspected by eye. Live e2e and privacy suites still pass.

Copy updated everywhere it needed to be: title, meta description, header, hero, trust row, a new "every sudoku has exactly one answer" section, a new FAQ, and the Product Hunt file — where the answer to "does it do sudoku?" had been "not yet".

## Phase 10 — mazes

Shipped and live (version `ad413fbe`). Three puzzle types now.

`src/generator/maze.js`: iterative randomised depth-first search — iterative because a 39×39 maze is 1521 cells deep in the worst case and recursion there is asking for trouble. Every maze is a spanning tree, so "every cell reachable, no loops, exactly one route from start to finish" is true by construction, not by inspection. The route comes from a breadth-first search, which in a tree is the only path as well as the shortest.

Performance never became a problem: expert is 6 ms a maze, and merging wall runs before drawing turns a 39×39 from roughly 3,000 line segments into 719 — a smaller PDF and a faster render. A 60-maze hard book is 693 KB.

**One real defect, found by looking at the output.** The first solutions page drew the route as a hairline in the same pale grey used for shading. At six mazes to a page it was invisible — an answer key that cannot be read is worse than no answer key, because the buyer only discovers it after printing. The route is now a wide grey corridor drawn under the walls, and it reads clearly at six-up.

Entrance and exit are holes in the border, deliberately kept out of the cell graph so the maze stays a clean spanning tree and `isPerfect` still means what it says; they exist only when walls are drawn. Pages are labelled "start" and "end" outside the walls.

**Verified:** 49 tests pass (was 42), 7 of them maze-specific — perfection at every difficulty, reciprocal openings (no one-way walls), the solution being a genuine adjacent-cell walk that never passes through a wall, size and route length increasing with difficulty, wall runs actually merging, the border actually being opened, determinism, and a 30-maze book with no repeats. Driven in a real browser across all three puzzle types: previews render, difficulty lists swap, a 60-maze book comes out at 74 pages, cover downloads, no overflow at 400 px, no console errors. Pages rasterised and inspected. Live e2e and privacy suites pass.

Copy updated: title, meta description, header, hero, trust row, a new "every maze has exactly one route" section, the puzzle-types FAQ, and the Product Hunt file.
