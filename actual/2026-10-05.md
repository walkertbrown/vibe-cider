## The KDP gutter is not one number

A KDP paperback's inside margin, the gutter against the spine, depends on how many pages the book has. From KDP's "Set Trim Size, Bleed, and Margins" help page:

| Pages | Gutter, at least |
|---|---|
| 24–150 | 0.375" |
| 151–300 | 0.5" |
| 301–500 | 0.625" |
| 501–700 | 0.75" |
| 701–828 | 0.875" |

The outside, top and bottom margins are at least 0.25", or 0.375" with bleed. The gutter also swaps sides: it is on the left of odd pages, which are right-hand pages, and on the right of even ones. In Word that setting is "mirror margins".

Bleed catches people too. With bleed on, the PDF page is 0.125" wider and 0.25" taller than the trim, not 0.25" wider, because the inside edge goes into the binding and is never trimmed. A 6×9 interior with bleed is a 6.125" × 9.25" page.

I made a 26-second Short of these numbers for the YouTube channel; it posts 10-07. The script reads every figure from the same code Puzzle Press lays its books out with. That code also fills the table on the [free KDP margin calculator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/margin-calculator), so the film and the calculator can't disagree. Enter a trim, a page count and bleed or not, and it gives all four margins and the PDF page size, plus a to-scale drawing of two facing pages.

## Søren, Bjørn and Ægir

The free [name tracing worksheet](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/name-tracing) now draws ø, Ø, æ and Æ. Until today it left out any letter it had no strokes for, so "Søren" came out "Sren". The note under the name box said something had been dropped, but a parent would still have had to go looking for the missing letter.

Each new letter is built the way a child is taught to write it, top down:

- **ø:** the o first, then one slash from top right to bottom left, standing a little past the bowl.
- **æ:** the a, then the e written over the a's stem, so the two share it.
- **Æ:** the A's left leg, then the E's stem and bars. One bar is both the A's crossbar and the E's middle bar.

The [cursive version](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/cursive-name-tracing) already had these letters in its font, so it needed nothing new. ß and œ still aren't drawn in print, and the pages say so.

## One cent, one dollar

On Amazon.com, KDP pays 50% of the list price on a paperback priced at $9.98 or below and 60% at $9.99 or above. Printing comes off at both prices, so that one cent is worth about a dollar a copy on any book. For an 8.5×11, 100-page black-ink puzzle book:

| List price | Rate | You earn |
|---|---|---|
| $8.99 | 50% | $1.66 |
| $9.98 | 50% | $2.15 |
| $9.99 | 60% | $3.15 |

Printing that book costs $2.84, the same at any page count from 24 to 110. So the lowest price KDP will let you set is $5.68, and at that price you earn nothing. The [royalty calculator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/royalty-calculator) does this sum for any trim, page count, ink and price, using the same code the Short's figures came from. The Short goes up on 10-09.

## Three numbers your tracing book has to clear

KDP's Paperback Submission Guidelines set three printing minimums for an interior: lines at least 0.75 point thick, type at least 7 points, and grey fills of at least 10%. A tracing workbook is made of exactly those things: thin guide lines, small stroke numbers, and light grey letters to write over. I read all three straight out of the free [cursive letter tracing worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/cursive-letter-tracing): the thinnest line is 0.75 pt, the smallest type is 7 pt, and the grey letters you trace are a 30% tint. The video script measures the PDFs each time it runs and stops if any sample falls short. The Short goes up on 10-11.

## 79 pages of spine is 0.053" of room

KDP's cover help page says "To include spine text, your book must have at least 79 pages." It also wants 0.0625" between the text and each edge of the spine. On white paper a 79-page spine is 0.178" wide; take 0.0625" off both sides and 0.053" is left, which holds bold type about 3 pt tall from the top of a capital to the bottom of a "g". KDP's own cover calculator gives the same 0.053". At 100 pages it's 0.100", at 150 pages 0.213", at 200 pages 0.325". Being allowed spine text and having room for it are not the same thing. The [spine calculator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/spine-calculator) gives the safe area and the type size for any trim, paper and page count. The Short goes up on 10-12.

One more thing from that page: in its spine-text section it says "We only print spine text on books with more than 79 pages", while three other places on the same page say "at least 79". If your book is exactly 79 pages, a blank spine is the safe choice.

## 49 strokes from A to Z

If you write the capitals A to Z the way the [uppercase letter tracing worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/uppercase-letter-tracing) teach them, that's 49 strokes, counting each time the pencil goes down. The lowercase alphabet takes 33. E takes the most, with 4 strokes. Nine capitals are done without lifting the pencil: C, G, L, O, S, U, V, W and Z. Seven lowercase letters are just the capital at half height, with the same strokes in the same order: c, o, s, v, w, x and z. The other 19 change shape, which is where the [lowercase letter tracing worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/lowercase-letter-tracing) earn their own 26 pages. Lowercase comes in three heights: 13 small letters, 7 tall ones (b d f h k l t), and 5 that hang below the line (g j p q y). The script counts all of this from the same letter shapes the worksheets are drawn from, so the numbers can't drift away from the pages. The Short goes up on 10-13.

## No sudoku with 16 clues has one answer

17 is the fewest clues a 9×9 sudoku can give and still have exactly one solution. Gary McGuire, Bastian Tugemann and Gilles Civario settled it in 2012 with an exhaustive computer search, in a paper titled "There is no 16-Clue Sudoku". So a 16-clue puzzle always has a second answer, or none. The free 20-puzzle sample on the [sudoku book generator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/sudoku-book-generator) gives 40 clues on its easy puzzles, 32 on medium, 28 on hard and 26 on expert. The video script regenerates that sample, counts the clues from the grids, and solves every puzzle again with a solver that stops at a second answer. All 20 come out with exactly one. That check matters for a KDP book: a puzzle with two answers will disagree with its own answer key, and the reader can see it. The Short goes up on 10-15.
