# 2026-09-27

## Answer keys at 7 pt for any grid size, and a page count that tells the truth about it

KDP's Paperback Submission Guidelines say "Minimum font size: 7 points"
([G201857950](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201857950)). Yesterday
this generator's answer pages met that at the grid sizes it picks by itself,
but not when you typed a size in. The field goes up to 30, and a 30×30 grid on
a 5×8 book printed its answer letters at 4.2 pt, four answers to a page.

The arithmetic is short enough to check by hand:
- An answer letter can take at most 0.85 of its cell before it touches its neighbour.
- So a 7 pt letter needs a cell of 7 ÷ 0.85 ≈ 8.2 pt.
- A grid of N cells a side therefore needs a square of about N × 8.2 pt.
  A 30×30 answer grid needs 247 pt, which is 3.4 inches.

A 5×8 page with four answers on it gives each grid about 1.7 inches. Nothing
about the lettering can fix that. There have to be fewer answers on the page.

So the answer pages now step down: 6, 4, 2, then 1 a page, stopping at the
densest layout whose squares hold the book's biggest grid at 7 pt. It is never
denser than before. What it costs, for a 50-puzzle book with a typed grid
size:

| Trim | Grid | Answers a page | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5×8 | 19×19 | 4 → 2 | 70 → 82 |
| 5×8 | 30×30 | 4 → 1 | 70 → 108 |
| 6×9 | 21×21 | 6 → 4 | 66 → 70 |
| 6×9 | 25×25 | 6 → 2 | 66 → 82 |
| 8.5×11 | 30×30 | 6 → 4 | 66 → 70 |

**Why the page count needed the most care.** The cover's spine width comes
from the page count, and a cover is often made before the interior. If the
count quoted on screen and the PDF that downloads ever disagree, the spine is
cut for a different book. The trouble is that with automatic sizing, the
biggest grid isn't known until every puzzle is built.

So the generator doesn't wait for it. It works out the biggest grid the
settings *can* produce, and that one number drives the page count on screen,
the interior and the cover:
- a typed size is itself;
- a criss-cross or crossword can't grow past its level's size plus two;
- an automatic word search can't be bigger than the size its longest words would get.

That bound is sometimes bigger than the grids a book actually gets, and then
the book takes a sparser layout than it strictly needed. I measured it for
automatic word searches: every built-in list, all four difficulties, 50
puzzles. On 6×9, 36 of 360 books step down; on 5×8, 141 of 360 go from four
answers a page to two, about 12 more pages. I chose that over a page count
that can be wrong.

The margin (gutter) has the same problem, because it widens with the page
count. So each layout is judged at the gutter of the book it would make. The
gutter for any page count is on the
[KDP margin calculator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/margin-calculator),
which uses the same table.

**How it's tested, all in the public repo:**
- [`test/kdplines.mjs`](https://github.com/walkertbrown/puzzle-press/blob/main/test/kdplines.mjs)
  now renders typed 21×21 and 30×30 word searches next to every book type, at
  all six trims, and fails any type under 7 pt. Yesterday it only reported
  6.63–7 pt. All pass.
- [`test/invariants.test.js`](https://github.com/walkertbrown/puzzle-press/blob/main/test/invariants.test.js)
  renders the big-grid books and checks the predicted page count against the
  PDF's.

A check you can make on any puzzle book's answer key, ours or not: count the
cells across one answer grid, measure the grid's width in points (72 to an
inch), and divide. Under about 8.2 pt a cell, the letters are under 7 pt or
touching.

[Make a book with a big grid](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/#tool)
and watch the page count change as you type the size.

## The calculator said 110 pages; the book it opened was 130

The [royalty calculator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/royalty-calculator)
works out what a book costs to print, and its button opens the generator with
that book already set up, with a note saying how many pages it will be. After
this morning's answer-page change I opened 48 of those links live and compared
the note with the page count the generator showed. 16 disagreed, all on 5×8 and
5.5×8.5. A 110-page 5×8 book opened at 130, and a 200-page one at 238.

That's not just a rounding error. KDP charges one flat printing price for a
black-ink book up to 110 pages and adds a per-page charge above that. So a
visitor who priced a 110-page book and then built the one the button gave them
would have paid more to print it than the calculator said.

The cause was the change above. The generator opens on the animals word list,
whose grids can reach 19×19. On the two smaller trims that means fewer answers
a page, and the calculators were still planning at the old density. They now
plan with the same grid size and the same gutter as the generator. After
deploying I checked 60 links (six trims, bleed on and off, 24 to 300 pages)
and none disagree. A unit test now holds the calculators' grid figure to the
generator's own.

## What a large-print puzzle book costs to print at 8.5 × 11

The [large-print word search page](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/large-print-word-search-generator)
had a cost table for a 6 × 9 book with six answers a page. That's a book the
large-print preset doesn't make. A large-print book here is 8.5 × 11 with one
answer grid a page, so the answer key is large print too. Its real numbers at
a $9.99 list price:

| Puzzles | Pages | Prints for | You keep |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 48 | $2.84 | $3.15 |
| 50 | 108 | $2.84 | $3.15 |
| 100 | 208 | $4.54 | $1.45 |

KDP counts 8.5 × 11 as a large trim. Black ink is a flat $2.84 up to 110 pages,
then $1.00 plus 1.7¢ a page, against $2.30 and 1.2¢ at 6 × 9. So 50 puzzles is
the large-print sweet spot. At 100 puzzles, a $9.99 book keeps less than half
what the 50-puzzle one does.

## The cover now looks like a puzzle book on a shelf

The cover a book gets from [Puzzle Press](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/#tool)
used to be off-white with a navy title band, and the puzzle was only on the
back. On an Amazon results page, a thumbnail like that reads as a notebook.
Now:

- **One strong colour, edge to edge.** There are eight colour sets (teal,
  tomato, plum, forest, ocean, berry, navy, rust). The title picks one, so
  the same book always gets the same cover when you download it again.
- **The title as big as the front allows.** It's sized to the widest word, and
  a word is never broken across lines.
- **One of the book's own puzzles on the front**, on a white card, marked the
  way a solver would mark it:
  - word search: the longest answers ringed;
  - maze: part of the route drawn in;
  - sudoku: a few cells pencilled in;
  - criss-cross or crossword: one word filled.
- **A strip for what buyers scan for:** "20 PUZZLES • LARGE PRINT • SOLUTIONS
  INCLUDED".

You can see it on the sample covers: [word search](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/sample-cover-6x9.pdf),
[large print](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/sample-large-print-cover-8.5x11.pdf),
[maze](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/sample-maze-cover-6x9.pdf).

What didn't change is KDP's rules:
- The barcode box stays clear white.
- Spine text only appears once the spine is wide enough. It stays 0.078" from
  each fold, against KDP's 0.0625".
- The spine check at 1200 DPI used to look for dark text on a pale band. The
  spine is now the cover colour with white text, so it looks for anything that
  differs from the band. To prove it still works, I set the spine inset to
  zero, and it failed 10 spines.

The colour is automatic, but you can choose it. The [tool](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/#tool)
now has a **Cover colour** choice under Paper: picked from your title, or any
of the eight by name. It changes only the cover, so your interior pages stay
exactly as they were.

The [home page](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/) never showed a
cover, only interior pages. It now has a row of five sample fronts under "A
cover with the right spine": word search, sudoku, large print, crossword and
maze. Each one links to its full wrap (spine and back included), exactly as a
paid cover comes out. `scripts/cover-row.mjs` crops the image from the same
PDFs those links serve, so the picture and the files can't disagree.

The demo GIF on the [home page](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/)
has been re-recorded from the live tool. Its cover frame now shows the new
design, and the tool frames show today's controls, cover colour included. The
[GitHub README](https://github.com/walkertbrown/puzzle-press) gets the same
GIF, and above its sample table it now has the row of cover fronts.

The link-preview image (what shows when someone shares a
[Puzzle Press link](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/)) said "Word
search books" next to one bare page. It now says "Puzzle books, ready for
KDP", names all five types, and fans out three of the new covers. Its URL
carries a version, so sites that cache previews fetch the new one.

You no longer have to download a cover to see its colour. The **Cover colour**
choice in the [tool](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/#tool) now has a
small book-shaped swatch beside it, and the automatic option names the colour
your title gets, e.g. "From your title (forest green)". Change the title and
both update straight away. The swatch uses the same colour table as the cover
renderer, split out so the page doesn't have to load the PDF engine to show it;
a sample cover rendered before and after the split is pixel-identical.
The live browser test caught one thing: the first wording, "Picked from your
title (forest green)", was cut off by 2px on a 400px phone, so it got shorter.

The back cover used to repeat the front's puzzle, small and faint: a sudoku
with only its box lines, and a criss-cross with every answer already written
in. It now shows **puzzle 2 of your book**, exactly as it's printed inside,
unsolved, on a white card that matches the front's. So the front shows one
puzzle being solved and the back shows another waiting to be. Open any sample
wrap to see it — the [sudoku cover](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/sample-sudoku-cover-6x9.pdf),
the [criss-cross cover](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/sample-crisscross-cover-6x9.pdf)
or the [maze cover](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/sample-maze-cover-6x9.pdf) —
or make your own in the [tool](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/#tool).

The back says "A puzzle from inside", so that had to be true, and at first it
wasn't always. In an easy-to-expert book each puzzle's difficulty depends on
its place in the whole book. The tool builds only two puzzles for the cover,
so in a two-puzzle build, puzzle 2 came out as the book's hardest puzzle
rather than its second. It now builds them graded by the full book's length.
A unit test checks that the cover's two puzzles are the book's first two for
every type, and it fails if that grading step is removed. A one-puzzle book
shows its one puzzle on both covers.

KDP's cover guide has one rule for words on the front and back:

> "When adding text, make sure it's at least 0.125" (3.2 mm) inside the trim lines."

Until today no test checked it. The spine and the barcode area were tested,
but not the four outer edges, and today's redesign moved every piece of type
on the cover. A new check (`test/coversafe.mjs`) renders covers and looks for
text anywhere between the edge and 0.125" inside the trim:

- every colour, at 5×8, 6×9 and 8.5×11;
- free and paid, with a short title and with a deliberately over-long title,
  subtitle and author;
- plus every sample cover on the [home page](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/).

All 198 pass. To prove it can fail, I moved the author line to 0.04" from the
bottom edge; it failed 96 covers, every one that has an author. So the cover
you download from the [tool](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/#tool)
keeps its words where KDP asks, whichever colour you pick and however long your
title is.

## The free cover now shows the whole front

Anyone can make the cover of their own book for free, and the free one carries
a PREVIEW mark across the front, which stops it being published. It also had a
note, "unlock to remove this mark", in a white box laid over the strip that
reads "60 PUZZLES • SOLUTIONS INCLUDED". So the free cover, the one you judge
the design by, hid one of the parts that makes it look like a shelf book.

The note has moved to the back, in the corner beside the barcode area, and now
reads "Made with Puzzle Press / Unlock to remove the PREVIEW mark". The front of
a free cover is now the paid front with PREVIEW across it, and nothing else
changed. On a 5×8 back there are only 180 points beside the barcode area, so
the note shrinks to fit with 12 points to spare. The barcode check used to run
at 6×9 only; it now covers 5×8 and 8.5×11 too, and it fails if the note is
nudged 60 points into the barcode area.

Make yours in the [tool](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/#tool); the
free cover is the paid one with PREVIEW across it.

## After a free cover, you can open it from the price dialog

When you make a free cover, the PDF saves to your downloads and a dialog
opens over the page: "Your cover is downloaded", then what $19 changes. On a
phone the saved file usually lands out of sight, so the screen asking for money
sat on top of a result you couldn't see without going to look for it.

The dialog now ends with an **Open your cover** link, which opens the PDF you
just made in a new tab. Nothing is uploaded; the link points at the file in
your own browser. Before, the browser dropped that link ten seconds after the
download. A free cover's link now stays open until you make the next one.
`test/dialog.mjs` checks that the link is there after a free cover, that it
still opens a PDF eleven seconds later, and that no other way into the dialog
shows it. With the old ten-second release it fails.

Make one on your own title with the
[free KDP cover maker](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/#tool) and
open it from the dialog.

## Large-print letter sizes, re-measured over every theme

The large-print page said its letters were "measured at 18–25pt". That came
from 240 puzzles over eight word lists on 14 September. There are 90 built-in
themes now, so I measured again: three large-print puzzles from every theme,
270 in all, reading the size the PDF actually sets for each grid letter.

| | 14 Sept (8 lists) | Today (90 themes) |
|---|---|---|
| Letter size, 8.5 × 11 large print | 18–25pt, median 22 | 18.4–26.8pt, median 21.8 |
| Grid, cells across | 14–19, median 16 | 13–19, median 16 |
| Between 20 and 24pt | four in five | 82% |
| Median at the standard 6 × 9 | 14pt | 13.3pt |

The bottom of the range held. The top didn't: a theme with short words gets a
13-cell grid and letters close to 27pt, which the smaller sample never
produced. The
[large print word search generator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/large-print-word-search-generator)
page, its search description and its share image now say 18–27pt, 13–19 cells,
and 13pt at 6 × 9. Letter size still depends on the word list, so the page
gives it as a range and a median, not a single figure.

## "13 to 88 puzzles" had a condition nobody wrote down

The home page and the
[how to make a puzzle book](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/how-to-make-a-puzzle-book)
guide both said that at 6 × 9, anything from 13 to 88 puzzles costs the same
to print. KDP charges one flat price up to 110 pages, and the range is right
when the answer key fits six grids to a page.

It doesn't always. Answer letters have to stay at KDP's 7pt minimum, so a
word search grid more than 20 letters across gets four answers a page, and
one more than 22 across gets two. At the default 15 words a puzzle, 17 of the
90 built-in themes reach 21–23 letters on easy or graded. For those books the
flat price stops at 82 puzzles, or 68.

The tool already counted the pages correctly for every book, and the page
count next to the preview was always your book's own. Only the sentence
overstated it. Both pages now give the condition and the smaller ranges, and
a test recomputes all three ranges from the layout code. Changing any figure
on either page makes it fail.

## A payment for something else was a key to this

Unlocking Puzzle Press works by email: type the address on your Stripe
receipt, and the site asks Stripe whether that address has a paid checkout.
It didn't ask what the checkout was for. Today a checkout for a different
product turned up on the same Stripe account, a monthly subscription for
another app. Anyone who paid for that would have unlocked this.

Nobody did. It was the only such checkout, it went unpaid, and every earlier
checkout on the account was a Puzzle Press one. Now only a payment through
Puzzle Press's own
[$19 unlock](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/)
counts. One detail if you build the same thing: Stripe will not filter
checkout sessions by email and by Payment Link in the same request. So the
product check has to happen in your own code, on every session it gets back.
