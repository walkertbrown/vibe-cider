# Actual — 2026-10-20

## Christmas: the date that matters is 30 October

To have a Christmas book on sale for Black Friday, 27 November 2026, a paperback that goes through KDP's low-content review needs submitting by **Friday 30 October**. That's four weeks of review counted back. Without the low-content box, KDP's 3 business days (up to 6 for a first book) put the last safe day at about Thursday 19 November. KDP also takes only 2 new paperbacks a week from each account. So a seasonal book takes one of that week's two slots.

A handwriting workbook can take the low-content box. The [Christmas tracing worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/christmas-tracing-worksheets) page opens Trace Press with twenty Christmas words. From there it makes a 46-page book in the browser: A to Z, then one word a page with a picture. It comes with its full-wrap cover, and there's no sign-up. A new short video shows exactly that, starting on the date.

## Puzzle Press: the free book's pages are no longer marked

Until today, a book made free with [Puzzle Press](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/) carried one small grey line in the footer of every page, and $19 removed it. That line is gone. A free interior is now the same PDF a paying customer gets: every puzzle, the solutions, page numbers and the inside margin sized to the page count. You can upload it to KDP and order a proof copy without paying anything.

What the $19 buys now is the cover. Puzzle Press makes the full-wrap cover with your title on it, with the spine sized from your book's page count. Free, that cover has PREVIEW across it; paid, it doesn't. The [comparison of seven puzzle-book tools](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/compare) is updated to say so. One tool on it, PuzzleForge, also gives unmarked puzzle pages for nothing, but it makes no cover at all.

## "Facebook" in your site's analytics may be Facebook itself

This week Trace Press's analytics named Facebook as its top source of visitors: 7 page loads, on phones and desktops. None was a person. Every one came from Meta's own network, in the same minute that Meta's link-preview fetcher took the page, the preview image and the free PDF. When someone pastes a link into Facebook or Messenger, Meta loads the page to draw the preview card. Part of that load runs in an ordinary-looking Chrome browser, which Cloudflare's web analytics counted as a visit referred by Facebook.

If you sell books or printables from your own site, check before you put time into Facebook because of that number. Look at the network the visits came from (Meta's addresses start 2a03:2880), and at whether those visitors did anything after the page loaded. Our counts on both sites now leave Meta out. What's left this week came from a handful of people, and none of them came from Facebook.
