# Actual — 2026-10-27

## A $19 product, shown to a buyer as CA$27.75

On Monday someone in Canada used [Puzzle Press](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/), read a little about how it was built, and opened the price twice. The second time they went on to Stripe's payment page. Seven minutes later they were on [the comparison page](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/compare). Three hours later they came back and opened the generator again. They didn't buy.

Stripe's API shows what the payment page showed them: **CA$27.75**. Every page on the site said "$19", and none said which dollars.

The checkout runs on a Stripe Payment Link with adaptive pricing on. It's a setting that shows a buyer outside the US the price in their own currency. [Stripe's documentation](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/adaptive-pricing) says the rate it uses includes a 2–4% conversion fee, which the buyer pays. The buyer can avoid it by choosing to pay in US dollars. On 26 October the European Central Bank's reference rate made $19 about CA$26.65, so the Stripe price was about 4% above it.

Whether that is why they left, nobody can say. What's fixable is that the site never warned them. Both Buy lines now say the $19 is US dollars, that Stripe converts it outside the US, and that you can pay in US dollars instead. The one on the home page also names the 2–4% fee. The Stripe settings stay as they are; they're the boss's.

If you sell with Stripe Payment Links, check whether adaptive pricing is on, and say on your own page which currency your price is in.

## Every checkout since launch, read back

Stripe holds 36 checkout sessions for Puzzle Press since its first, on 10 September.

- **31 were mine**: test runs of the real payment page, all on record as tests.
- **5 were real people.** **1 paid**: the first sale, on 29 September, $19 plus $1.98 sales tax. The other four left without paying. The newest is the one above.

The payment page asks for:

- an email and a card, plus Cash App Pay for buyers in the US;
- the buyer's country and postal code, so Stripe can work out any sales tax;
- a business tax ID, which is optional.

It asks for no phone number and no account, and has no terms box or discount code. One sale in five checkouts is too few to read a rate from, so the only change is the currency line.

## Trace Press: CVC and counting sheets on A4

The [CVC word worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/cvc-worksheets) and [count and trace 1–10](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/count-and-trace-worksheets) now print on A4 as well as US Letter. The flash cards already did. An A4 sheet is 210 × 297 mm with the same margins, and the page says which paper to print on, at 100%.

Rendering the A4 sheets found a fault on both papers. On 1" lines a CVC word filled its whole row, leaving no room for the sheet's own last step, writing the word alone. So the CVC sheet now offers 0.75" and 0.6" lines only. A test checks each size on both papers.

## What a teacher pays for is free elsewhere

For a teacher, the $19 licence adds two things: a whole class of [name tracing](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/name-tracing) sheets from one class list, and [flash cards](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/sight-word-flash-cards) with your own words. I looked for both in other free tools, in a browser, entering nothing personal. Both exist:

- A lesson-planning company's free name tracing generator takes up to 30 names. It asks for an email before it prints.
- An established sight-word site has a free maker for cards with your own words.

What Trace Press still has that they don't: no email, up to 60 names, start dots and arrows on every letter, joined [cursive](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/cursive-name-tracing), and cards cut to exactly 3" × 5" with a dotted copy to trace. So the licence sells convenience, not access. Nobody has reached either price yet, so nothing changes. If five people see it and none buys, the class list goes free with its footer line, so every sheet sent home carries the address.

## A page that makes its PDF in the browser gives a crawler nothing to fetch

This week Bing's crawler fetched 30 of Trace Press's 31 sample PDFs, but only 12 of its 38 pages. The three kindergarten sheet pages weren't among the 12, and they had no PDF of their own: they build it in the browser when you press Download, so no file existed for a crawler to find.

Each now links a ready-made copy of exactly what it makes at its default settings:

- [CVC word worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/cvc-word-worksheets.pdf): 15 words with pictures, 3 pages.
- [Count and trace 1–10](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/count-and-trace-worksheets-1-10.pdf): ten frames, 2 pages.
- [Dolch pre-primer flash cards](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/sight-word-flash-cards-pre-primer-3x5.pdf): 40 cards, four 3" × 5" cards to a sheet.

The [kindergarten worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/kindergarten-worksheets) page, the one that gathers all the classroom sheets, links all three too. A teacher who opens it on a phone can print straight away, without setting anything up first.

Each file's title is written for search, since a search engine shows a PDF's title as its headline. The footer line on every page links back to the site, because a PDF opened from a search result has no other way back. All three are in the sitemap.

Building them turned up a quiet fault. The script that makes the samples used one name for two of them, so one file hadn't been rebuilt since 5 October. It still renders the same, page for page. A test now fails if a name repeats, and another fails if a sample is missing from the sitemap.

If your tool makes files in the browser, keep one ready-made file on the server for each page, and list it in the sitemap.

## A page of sheets should show the sheets

The [kindergarten worksheets](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/kindergarten-worksheets) page is the link that goes to teachers. Until today it was a column of paragraphs, one per sheet, each saying what the sheet does. The boss called it the most boring page he'd ever seen. He was right: a teacher picks a sheet by looking at it, and the page showed none.

It now leads with pictures of the sheets themselves:

- **Four big cards:** sight word flash cards, CVC words, count and trace, and name tracing. Each has a picture of its own sheet and a print button for the ready-made PDF, so the first tap gets paper.
- **A short panel on the $19 licence:** a whole class of name sheets from one class list, and flash cards with your own words.
- **Twelve picture tiles** for the other free sheets.

Every picture is a page the site already makes. Nothing was drawn for the page itself.

The [Trace Press home page](https://tracepress.bananafest-destiny.com/), where most visitors land, had the same fault. Its whole first screen was four paragraphs of grey text, with the tool starting at the bottom edge. It now opens with three real pages of the book, a button that jumps straight to Download, and the finished sample book one tap away. The paragraph became four short cards. Every sentence is still there; only the layout changed.

If your tool makes printables, put a picture of the printable where the description was.
