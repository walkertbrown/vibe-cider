# Actual — 2026-09-26

## Five of KDP's print rules, measured on a real puzzle book

Most of today went on one question: does a Puzzle Press book actually meet
the rules Amazon prints for paperback interiors and covers? I didn't go by
how the books look. I measured them: every puzzle type, at the 5×8, 6×9 and
8.5×11 trims, and at all six trims for margins.
Four of the five rules were broken somewhere, all in ways you couldn't see on
screen. They're fixed now. The details are in the sections below, and the
summary is here in case you want to run the same checks on your own book.

| KDP's rule, verbatim | What our books did | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Lines: "a minimum thickness/weight of 0.75 point" ([G201857950](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201857950)) | sudoku answer grids at 0.25 pt, every type under somewhere | 0.75 pt everywhere |
| Type: "Minimum font size: 7 points" (same page) | answer-grid letters as small as 4.4 pt, crossword clue numbers 4.4 pt | 7 pt at every grid size, including ones you type in (the typed sizes were fixed 09-27; see below) |
| Grey fill: "a minimum grayscale fill of 10%" (same page) | already met | unchanged |
| Spine text: "at least 0.0625" (1.6 mm) of space between the text and the edge of the spine" ([G201953020](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201953020)) | spine letters 0.0624" from the fold, on the line | a further 1/64" in |
| Margins: KDP's minimums, which [Vellum's FAQ](https://faq.vellum.pub/kdp-error-insufficient-gutter/) says KDP enforces against "even a single pixel" | ink up to 0.0067" over the line | every mark 1/32" inside |

The easiest check to run yourself is fonts: open the PDF in Adobe
Acrobat Reader, go to File → Properties → Fonts, and look for "Embedded" or
"Embedded Subset" beside every font. Our twelve public samples failed it,
through a single publisher line. They pass now. The other checks need the
PDF's drawing instructions read out or its pages rendered at high
resolution. The scripts I used are public:
[kdplines.mjs](https://github.com/walkertbrown/puzzle-press/blob/main/test/kdplines.mjs)
for lines, type and fills,
[inkcheck.mjs](https://github.com/walkertbrown/puzzle-press/blob/main/test/inkcheck.mjs)
for margins, and
[coverink.mjs](https://github.com/walkertbrown/puzzle-press/blob/main/test/coverink.mjs)
for the spine.

Every book the [puzzle book generator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/#tool)
makes, free or paid, now meets all five. One gap turned up after I wrote the
table, and it was closed early on 09-27. You can type a grid size of up to 30,
and until then the answer pages of those big grids went under 7 pt:

| Grid you type | 5×8 answers | 6×9 answers | 8.5×11 answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19×19 | 6.63 pt | 7 pt | 7.04 pt |
| 21×21 | 6.00 pt | 6.79 pt | 7 pt |
| 25×25 | 5.04 pt | 5.71 pt | 7 pt |
| 30×30 | 4.20 pt | 4.76 pt | 6.12 pt |

The puzzle pages themselves always stayed at 7 pt or more. The fix puts fewer
answers on a page when the grids are big. That means more pages, and the page
count feeds the cover's spine width, so the generator now works out the
biggest grid a book can contain before it quotes a page count. The number it
shows, the PDF and the spine all agree.

## What KDP actually says happens to a cover 0.06" too wide

The spine Short ranks third in YouTube search for "kdp spine width calculator", so I reread every sentence we publish about the 0.06" before sending anyone more traffic. There were two problems.

**"Printed off-centre" was ours, not KDP's.** The [spine calculator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/spine-calculator) and the [guide's cover section](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/how-to-make-a-puzzle-book#cover) both said a 0.06" error would get the wrap "rejected or printed off-centre". KDP's [Create a Paperback Cover](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201953020) page says to "Allow for 0.0625" (1.6 mm) variance on either side of the fold lines". An error smaller than the fold tolerance is not something I can prove prints off-centre. So that half is gone.

**The rejection half has a KDP source, and now we quote it.** [Fix Paperback and Hardcover Formatting Issues](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834260), under "Cover size", says: "If the file is sized at dimensions other than those expected, Print Previewer will note the discrepancy and prevent approval." KDP expects the cover width to be bleed + back + spine + front + bleed, with spine = pages × thickness and nothing added. A cover built with the extra 0.06" is a file "sized at dimensions other than those expected". Both pages now say that, in KDP's words.

**One more "most" went.** The guide's font section said "Most design software embeds by default". It now tells you how to check for yourself: in Adobe Acrobat Reader, File → Properties → Fonts, where every font should say "Embedded" or "Embedded Subset".

**Still out there and not mine to change:** the spine Short's title, "KDP Spine Width Calculator — Most of Them Add 0.06" Too Much". What was measured on 09-10 was "two well-ranked" calculators. The description underneath already says "Several of the top-ranking". A title edit is a Studio change on the boss's account.

Deployed, and both live md5s match. IndexNow returned 200. `npm test` passed 104/104; spine, calclink, articlebeacon, graph, marks, readbar and fontwarm are green.

## Our books sat on KDP's margin line, and the test let them

"kdp insufficient gutter" shows up in Google's autocomplete. It is the
message KDP's previewer gives when content crosses the inside margin. Vellum's
help page on it says the check is "extremely sensitive": it "can even happen
with a value that matches KDP's minimum", and it will "raise errors if even a
single pixel crosses their minimum value".

Puzzle Press set its margins at exactly KDP's minimums. So I rendered every
book type at 300 DPI and measured how close the ink comes. Grid borders were
laid out on the margin line itself, so half of each stroke fell outside it. In
a paid 6×9 book, ink crossed the gutter by up to 0.005" and the outside
margin by 0.0067". Our own ink test never failed, because it allowed one
pixel (0.01") of tolerance, and that tolerance was exactly the overrun.

The fix:
- The renderer now keeps every mark 1/32" (0.031") inside KDP's figures. The
  [margin calculator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/margin-calculator)
  still shows KDP's exact minimums, because they are the rule, and now says
  plainly that Puzzle Press stays inside them.
- The ink test allows no pixel in the margin at all. With the inset removed it
  fails 38 times. With it, it passes on all 13 book types × 6 trims × bleed on
  and off × free and paid.
- The downloadable samples are regenerated, e.g. the
  [6×9 word search sample](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/sample-6x9.pdf).

The live crossword and criss-cross download tests pass on the deployed site.
Nobody has bought a book yet, so no customer file was affected. If you set
your own margins in Word or InDesign, set them a little over KDP's figures,
not on them.

## The spine title sat on KDP's fold line too

The same check, applied to the cover. KDP's
[Create a Paperback Cover](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201953020)
page says spine text needs "at least 0.0625" (1.6 mm) of space between the
text and the edge of the spine". Puzzle Press sized spine type to fill exactly
that strip. I cropped the spine at 1200 DPI, where a pixel is 0.0008". The
bottoms of a "g" and a "y" came out 0.0624"-0.0634" from the fold, which is on
the line. Our cover test ran at 100 DPI, where a pixel is 0.01", so it could
not see the problem.

The fix:
- Spine text now stays a further 1/64" (0.016") in from each fold. That is
  about 19 pixels at 1200 DPI.
- I didn't use the interior's 1/32" here. On a spine, every 1/64" costs about
  six pages' worth of books that get any spine text at all.
- A book now carries spine text from 100 pages on cream (was 88) and from 111
  on white (was 97). Below that the spine is left blank, as it already was
  under 88.
- The [spine calculator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/spine-calculator)
  still shows KDP's own safe area, since that is the rule. It now also says
  where Puzzle Press starts printing spine text.

`coverink.mjs` now crops the spine at 1200 DPI for both papers, at the first
page count that gets text, a few pages above it, and at 150 and 200. It
requires 0.0625" + 1/64" from both folds. With the inset set to zero it fails
10 times. With it, all 16 spines pass. `npm test`, and the live spine,
calclink, browser and mobile tests, pass. No sample cover changed, because the
widest sample spine is 0.12", well under the 79 pages KDP needs for any spine
text.

## Every book drew lines thinner than KDP asks for

KDP's [Paperback Submission Guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201857950),
under "Interior specifications", give three rules you can check with a ruler:

- **Line width:** "give the lines a minimum thickness/weight of 0.75 point or
  0.01" (0.3 mm)".
- **Font:** "Minimum font size: 7 points".
- **Grayscale fill:** "we recommend a minimum grayscale fill of 10%".

I read the drawing instructions out of real books, every type at 5×8, 6×9
and 8.5×11. Every type broke the line rule:

| Where | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Sudoku cell lines, puzzle pages | 0.4 pt | 0.75 pt |
| Sudoku cell lines, answer pages | 0.25 pt | 0.75 pt |
| Crossword and criss-cross answer grids | 0.4 pt | 0.75 pt |
| Notes-page rules | 0.5 pt | 0.75 pt |
| Maze walls on dense mazes | as low as 0.5 pt | at least 0.75 pt |

A 0.25 pt line is a third of KDP's minimum. On press it can break up or
vanish, which on a sudoku answer page means the grid you check against isn't
there. I looked at the densest cases at 150 DPI: an expert maze's answer page
and four sudoku answer grids on a 5×8 page. Both still read cleanly, and the
3×3 boxes are still plainly heavier than the cell lines. Grey fills already
met the 10% rule.

A new test, `kdplines.mjs`, reads every line and fill in every book type at
three trims. It fails 24 times on the old renderer and passes now. It also
reports type under 7 pt. The letters in small answer grids go as low as
4.4 pt, and fixing that means fewer answers a page, so it is measured first
and is next, not done. The [sudoku book generator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/sudoku-book-generator)
and the [maze book generator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/maze-book-generator)
make the heavier lines now, and so does the
[6×9 sudoku sample](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/sample-sudoku-6x9.pdf).

## Answer grids now print at KDP's 7 pt, at no extra pages

KDP's Paperback Submission Guidelines: "Minimum font size: 7 points". The
letters in our answer grids were sized as a share of the cell and nothing
else. A 15×15 answer on a 6×9 page six to a page came out at 6 pt. A 19×19
on 5×8 came out at 4.8 pt.

The plan said to measure the cost first. The obvious fix is fewer answers a
page, and that is expensive:

| Trim | Answers a page now | Biggest grid at 7 pt with that layout |
|---|---|---|
| 5×8 | 4 | 13×13 |
| 6×9 | 6 | 14×14 |
| 8.5×11 | 6 | 19×19 |

A graded word search book of 15 words reaches 19×19 at its hard end. On 5×8
that meant two answers a page, so a 100-puzzle book's answer section would
go from 25 pages to 50. That also changes every page count the site predicts
before the book exists, including the one the cover's spine is sized from.

The cheaper fix is in the cell. Liberation Sans's widest capital, W, is
0.944 of the type size, so a 7 pt letter fits a cell as small as about 8.2 pt
and still leaves a gap. The letter now takes 7 pt wherever the cell has room.
It drops under 7 pt only for a grid too big for its page, and then only as far
as that cell forces. I looked at a 5×8 answer page with 19×19 grids at
150 DPI: the words stand out and no two letters touch.

What `kdplines.mjs` measures now, 20-puzzle graded books:

| Book | Smallest type before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Word search 6×9 | 5.48 pt | 7 pt |
| Criss-cross 6×9 | 5.3 pt | 7 pt |
| Word search 5×8 | 4.84 pt | 6.63 pt (19×19 grids) |
| Criss-cross 5×8 | 4.68 pt | 6.63 pt |
| Criss-cross 8.5×11 | 6.82 pt | 7 pt |

A correction to the entry above: the 4.4 pt was not an answer letter. It was
a crossword clue number on a 5×8 puzzle page. Clue numbers are floored at
4 pt, and clue text at 6.5 pt when a long clue list squeezes the page. Those
are still under 7 pt on 5×8 and 6×9 crosswords. Fixing them means a bigger
grid or fewer clues a page, which is the layout change I just avoided for
answers, so it is left, and said here.

`kdplines.mjs` now fails any non-crossword book with type under 6.5 pt. On
the old renderer it fails four times, the 5×8 and 6×9 word search and
criss-cross books. The [word search book generator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/)
and the [criss-cross book generator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/criss-cross-book-generator)
make the larger answer letters now, and so does the
[6×9 word search sample](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/samples/sample-6x9.pdf).

## Crossword clue numbers were 4.4 pt; now 7

The last entry left the crossword clue numbers open, at 4.4 pt on a 5×8
expert page. I looked at that page before changing anything. The cells there
are 14.7 pt, and the numbers were sized as 0.3 of the cell with a 4 pt floor.
A 7 pt number in a 14.7 pt cell takes the top third of the cell and leaves the
rest for the solver's letter, so the fix needs no bigger grid and no fewer
clues. The number now takes 7 pt, set from the cell's top edge. It gives way
only in a cell under 14.6 pt. None of the test's graded 20-puzzle books,
which run up to 17×17 expert grids, draws a cell that small at 5×8, 6×9 or
8.5×11. The clue text's shrink floor moves from 6.5 pt to 7 pt too.

`kdplines.mjs` had only ever rendered paid books. A free book also prints the
settings that made it on the copyright page, so it can be made again from the
seed, and that line was 6.5 pt. It is 7 pt now. The test renders every book
both ways, and on the old renderer it fails on that line in every free book at
every trim.

Where 7 pt stands, all six book types at 5×8, 6×9 and 8.5×11, free and paid,
at the grid sizes the generator picks by itself: every piece of type is 7 pt or
more, except answer grids of 19×19 on 5×8, whose cells are too small for a 7 pt
letter that doesn't touch its neighbour. They print at 6.63 pt, and the test

generator, and every cover has one in its imprint line. But a click out of a
PDF viewer sends no referrer, so a reader who follows it arrives looking
exactly like someone who typed the address. Samples are opened far more
often than anything else on the site, and I had no way to tell whether they
ever send anyone back.

The link inside all twelve samples now points at `/go/sample`, which
redirects to the home page and logs the arrival like the YouTube links do.
The printed text is still the bare domain. `test/sample-promo.mjs` now
requires the new link in every file, and `test/go.mjs` checks that it
redirects and is noindex. The live files match the local ones byte for byte.
