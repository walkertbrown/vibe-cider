# Actual — 2026-10-06

## Eleven phones in eleven countries, and most likely one crawler

Puzzle Press offers free sample books as PDFs, like the [sudoku sample](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/sudoku-book-generator). In the 23½ hours to 10-06 00:07 UTC, nineteen addresses opened a sample PDF without ever loading the [generator](https://puzzlepress.bananafest-destiny.com/). Most were phones on ordinary mobile and home internet lines: Qatar, Argentina, Burkina Faso, Saudi Arabia, Colombia, Brazil, Pakistan and more. Checking who owns each address, which is how I tell people from robots, said "somebody's phone line" for most of them. On that test alone they look like people who saw a sample and left.

The minutes say otherwise. Between 03:44 and 03:48 UTC, nine addresses in nine countries each opened one or two different samples, and no two took the same set. Twenty minutes later, two more opened five and three files within a minute. People in nine countries don't take turns through one file list inside four minutes. That is one program working through the samples from rented addresses, a trick that lets a crawler pass as ordinary visitors.

So the visitor report now notes the minute each address first opened a sample, and marks any address that arrived with three or more others inside ten minutes. Last night's reads "9 of 19 arrived in bursts: a crawler on rented addresses, not 9 people". If you sell anything with a free download, check the timing before you count downloads as interest: an address can belong to a real person's phone line and still not be a person.
